With an MSE loss, the model learns to preduct the point that minimises the sum of squared distances to all training examples. Relating this to the idea of a centroid in clustering, this means the model learns to output the average of all possible images that could correspond to a noisy input, and results are the average of many images seen by the model. Thus, the expected results are a blur of all ambiguous possible digit shapes rather than sharp, distinguishable digits. This is verified by the actual output, where the "clean" images produced looks like many digits all at once. (8,9,3,5,0 etc.)

Part B2: Training a Flow Matching Model

The one-step denoising is not effective for generative tasks, so this part focuses on iteratively denoising the image with flow matching, a multi-step procedure that gradually morphs noise into a clean image.

Flow matching works by starting with a pure noise image x0, a real MNIST image x1, and for a time t between 0 and 1, define an in-between image x_t as a linear interpolation between x0 and x1. When t=0, x_t is noise and when t=1, x_t is a clean image. When t is between 0 and 1, x_t is a mix of the two images. Flow is essentially velocity and determines how the images should move if time increments bit by bit. The goal is to train UNet to approximate this flow/velocity, such that when given a partially-noisy image x_t at time t, the UNet can predict the direction you should move the image to go closer to x1.

Part B2.1-2.2: Adding Time Conditioning to UNet and Training

Previously, the UNet took just an image as input. Now, it also needs to know what the value of t is, as the correct flow depends on how far the path already is from noise to data.

The training loop this time is to sample a real MNIST image x1 and a random noise x0 from a Gaussian, and pick a random t in [0,1]. Compute the in-between image x_t and true flow u, then feed x_t and t into time-conditioned UNet. Train the model to output u until model converges. Part B2.4-2.5: Adding Class-Conditioning to UNet and Training

In addition to x_t and t, the model is fed another input called class label c(0-9). c is represented as a one-hot vector (length 10, all zeroes except 1 in the position of the data), and more FCBlocks are used to turn t and c into vectors (t1,c1,t2,c2). They are combined with the UNet feature maps. However, since we want the UNet to work with or without the class conditioning, c is set to 0 for 10% of the training duration.

Removing the exponential learning rate scheduler but keeping performance similar by tweaking other parameters, the results after training without scheduler are visualised:

To compensate for the loss of the scheduler, I used a constant learning rate of 3e-3 throughout the 10 epochs of training, no other hyperparameter tuning was done. This is because the original exponential learning rate scheduler starts at a high learning rate then decays to a low one, which is compensated by taking a constant middle-ground learning rate, calculated using the geometric mean of the start and end learning rate of the scheduler. Qualitatively, the results without the scheduler are comparable to those with the scheduler.
